Henkerswald did his name proud there. It was unnervingly eerie. Almost ghostly. A narrow forest path meandered through the forest that had not been managed for many years. No timber was felled and no seedlings were planted, nor did anyone care about the damage caused by storms or other weather events. Uprooted trees remained untouched, and nobody cleared fallen branches to one side. Nature simply left itself be, and over time a small primeval forest had emerged. The mighty old trees - mostly oaks, beeches, pines, and firs - stood close together and stretched high towards the grey afternoon sky, which occasionally shone through the treetops. The thick trunks were covered with dense moss and draped in ivy. Loose lichens and various climbing plants hung from many branches. From the ground covered by withered leaves sprouted metre-high ferns whose fronds had turned a grey-brown color.
