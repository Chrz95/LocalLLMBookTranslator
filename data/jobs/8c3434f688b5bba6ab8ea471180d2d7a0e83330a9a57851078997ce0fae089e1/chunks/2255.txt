Hoppel, who was only called Alexander by his classmates, sat right next to Pickel-Paule. Although he usually did quite well in math and had a solid three on the subject, this test posed significant problems for him. He had managed to solve barely half of the tasks and had no idea how to approach the rest. In desperation, he tried to sneak a peek at his neighbor's paper. That’s why he occasionally cast surreptitious glances at Pickel-Paule’s sheet, which was almost finished with the work. When Pickel-Paule noticed Hoppel’s attempt at copying, he tilted his chair sideways, moved his paper to the far left edge of his desk, and shielded it further by using his arm and shoulder, making it impossible for Hoppel to copy anymore. Unless Hoppel stretched his head and leaned conspicuously towards him – but of course, even that would be noticed by the Taxus.
