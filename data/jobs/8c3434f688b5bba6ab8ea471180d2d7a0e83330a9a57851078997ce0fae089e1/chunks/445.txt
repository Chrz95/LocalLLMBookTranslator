As the car drove over a hilltop, Burg Ravenstein was already visible in the distance. It had been built on the highest elevation of the land and thus was easily seen from afar. Ravenstein was not a powerful fortress nor an impressive castle, but rather small and unassuming. Yet within this place, which was occasionally covered with ivy, everything that belonged to a proper castle could be found: tall towers, a drawbridge, and a moat, grim dungeons, dark cellars, and winding secret passages. It was even said that there was a torture chamber deep underground with the appropriate instruments. But so far no "Ravensteiner," as the intern students were generally called, had seen it.
